Those blocks already exist. They are built, people are living in them, and the
clock on each one is already running. So the question I keep getting is a fair
one: should I sell now, before all of that hits the market?

The honest answer is that it depends on you, not on the number.

## If you're in a rush, sell before the flood

If you need to move — new job, growing family, whatever it is — then yes, get
ahead of it. Once those flats reach MOP in your area, there is going to be
oversupply, and you will be one of many. Being early is worth something.

## If you're not in a rush, waiting can work in your favour

Here is the part people don't expect. MOP flats usually peak right at the MOP
month. The lease is at its maximum, the flat is as new as it will ever be
again, and buyers know it. So waiting doesn't automatically hurt you. It can
push your price up.

Two people, same block, same flat type, opposite advice. That's not me hedging.
It's that "should I sell" is a question about your own timeline, and most
articles about supply forget to ask it.

## What most agents leave out

You will hear this a lot: the moment your flat reaches MOP, sell it, because
that's when you get the highest price.

To a certain extent that's true. At MOP the remaining lease is at its maximum,
and lease matters. But the price of your flat is not only determined by the
lease remaining. It is also determined by **demand at that point in time**.

That second half is the part nobody says out loud, and it's the part that
actually decides whether you get your number.

## A client I couldn't help

I had a client whose flat reached MOP. They wanted to sell, but they were on the
fence — sell now, or later. So they set a price that wasn't realistic.

We got an offer. It was higher than the average in the market at the time. A
good offer. They turned it down, because by then they had decided the agent
wasn't doing the job properly — other agents had promised they could get the
price they had in their head.

They went with one of those agents. The flat never sold.

I think about that one a lot. The market gave them a real answer and they
preferred the promise.

## So where are we actually?

Not crashing. Not hot either.

{{index}}

Flat, year on year, while everyone talks about it being one extreme or the
other.

My read: the recent launches did exceptionally well, and the buyers who were
actively looking have already bought something. So there's a stagnation right
now. People are watching.

That doesn't last forever. Singapore is small — there are always people buying
and selling houses. It's just that right now, a lot of them are waiting for
something.

The problem is that the market has shifted and seller expectations haven't. That
makes this a seller's problem more than a buyer's opportunity. Properties are
not moving as quickly, but the prices people have in their heads are still from
a different market.

## About Tengah, since people keep asking

Tengah has 14,148 units in the pipeline and not one resale on record. That is
not owners refusing to sell, and it is not a market signal.

It's a brand new estate. The earliest block there doesn't reach its fifth year
until **2028**. Nothing in Tengah is eligible to be sold yet, so of course
nothing has sold.

Worth saying plainly, because an empty column in a dataset is very easy to read
as something it isn't.

## What I'd actually do

Work out whether you are in a rush. Genuinely — not "I'd move if the price was
right", but whether your life needs you to move in the next year.

If yes, go early and price to the market you are in, not the one you remember.

If no, you have the luxury of waiting for your own MOP peak, and you should use
it. But go in knowing that lease is only half the equation, and the other half —
demand — is not something you or I control.

Either way, start with what your own block has actually been transacting at. Not
the town, not the national index. Your block.
