A seller's agent may tell my buyer that someone else is coming back for a second viewing. A busy open house can create the same feeling: there are other people in the room, so perhaps we need to decide now. The agent may be telling the truth. But a second viewing is interest, not an offer, and a room full of viewers is not evidence of what this unit will sell for.

I would slow that moment down. What did we actually see in the unit — condition, noise, light, the parts no transaction record can show? How does the asking price sit against filed sales of genuinely comparable homes? Is there a real offer or deadline, or only a statement that other people are looking? Those are different questions, and pressure does not answer any of them.

If the home still makes sense after those checks, we can decide what we are comfortable offering. If it does not, the sight of other viewers should not set our limit for us. An open house tells us how the sale is being presented; it does not tell us what the property is worth to this buyer.
