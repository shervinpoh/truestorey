# Blindspot first-use study

This is the next buyer-facing validation pass, not a claim that five buyers
have already tested the site. Do not substitute an agent's walkthrough for
their behaviour.

## Who and how

Watch five Singapore buyers who have not been shown the site before. Ask them
to use their own phone and a listing they are genuinely considering. Aim for
both HDB and private-home searches. Do not collect their names, contact
details, exact listing URL or screen recording by default; note task outcomes
and de-identified quotes. If any session is recorded, ask for explicit
permission first.

Give only this task: **“You are deciding whether to view this home. Use this
site to find what you would check before committing to it.”** Start at the
homepage. The participant may refer to their listing for the asking price and
floor area. Do not name Blindspot or explain the score unless they ask what a
word means; write the question down before answering.

Observe, rather than teach:

1. Can they say what the site does, in their own words, before touching it?
2. Do they find the property search and understand why the address is needed?
3. Can they enter the listing price and area and finish the check unaided?
4. Do they distinguish a flagged check, an unmeasured check and a check that
   does not apply? Ask: “What would you do with this result?”
5. Can they identify one viewing question and keep or send the result?
6. Where do they hesitate, backtrack, dismiss copy, or assume a score means a
   property is safe or unsafe?

Record one row per participant: property type; completed unaided (yes/no);
first hesitation; interpretation of points; next action they chose; whether
they could keep/share; one verbatim de-identified quote. Do not average five
sessions into a conversion rate. Fix repeated misunderstandings first, then
run another round with new people.

## Internal walkthrough, 23 Sep — not user research

At a 390px viewport, a public HDB block could be found, supplied with a sample
asking price and area, and checked. The completed report displayed the weighted
points and denominator, three viewing questions, and its filed comparable
cohort without horizontal overflow. A copied link reopened the property and
re-ran the report. Editing the floor area previously left the old report below
the new input; the form now clears that stale result and the old link fragment.

Two questions remain for buyers, not this walkthrough, to settle: whether the
search-only first screen explains the value strongly enough when a reader has
no listing ready, and whether “risk points” is understood as a checklist rather
than a verdict. Do not change those labels on an internal hunch alone.
