The property

Type an address. Everything else fills itself in from what that block has actually filed — change anything that is wrong.

    Client-safe drops the point estimate and keeps the range and the comparables — the shape rule 2 permits on anything that leaves this screen.

    Can they afford it?

    TDSR, MSR, LTV and the stress rate, from lib/calc. Every rate carries a source and a review date.

    What would a seller clear?

    The CPF refund with accrued interest is the number sellers are most often wrong about — and it is the one that decides whether there is cash at the end.

    Blocks trading away from their neighbourhood

    Every HDB block priced twice — once on its own filed sales, once on its neighbours' — with the part visible risk explains already stripped out. This is a farming list, not listings. It says where to look.

    Condominiums against their own district

    Every condominium resale priced against what its district says a home of that size, tenure, remaining lease and storey should fetch. The same farming list, for private. What is left after all of that is the part the fit was never given — address, build, outlook — so read it as a map of which buildings carry a premium, not as a list of mispricings.

    Screen listings

    Paste listings — one per block, separated by a blank line. A link, copied detail, or both. Nothing is fetched from a portal; this reads what you paste.

    What your feed has been doing

    Built by npm run ingest:listings, one snapshot at a time. A single export is a list; a run of them tells you what has been sitting and what has been cut — which is the only reason to ingest weekly.

    Side by side

    Up to four. Each is priced on its own evidence — nothing here is averaged across them.

      What does this export cover?

      There is no default. Pick full by mistake on a filtered export and every listing it didn't cover disappears, which looks exactly like the market moving.

      What the tools are running on

      Every tool reads a file somebody had to build. An old one gives confident answers about a market that has moved — and nothing inside the tool can tell.

      Today's reading

      Headlines and links from the publishers' own feeds. Click through and read it at the source — nothing of the article is held here beyond the one-liner they put in the feed themselves.

      Where the indices stand

      The numbers every chain on this page ends in. Quarterly, and they lag whatever caused them by more than one quarter.

      If the rate moves

      The two consequences people routinely merge: what a borrower pays, which moves, and what they may borrow, which often does not.

      How it reaches Singapore

      This is not a news feed. Nothing here reproduces anyone's reporting — the chains are written down once and reviewed like a rate, the primary sources are linked, and what changes between readings is the data at each link. The part usually missing from commentary is the third column: what a lever does not touch.

      Profile a development

      Not one unit priced — the whole thing, compared with itself. Holding one project constant is what makes its own size and floor spreads readable: location, tenure, vintage, facilities and developer are identical on both sides of every comparison below.

        What a launch has to price at

        A new launch is priced off its land, not off the resales beside it. URA publishes what every GLS site was awarded for; this joins those awards to what the project on them actually launched at, and measures everything in between — construction, finance, marketing and profit — instead of taking it from a cost guide.
